# Operation Lifeline: Supply Chain Crisis Lab

**A realistic, interactive supply chain crisis management simulator designed for beginners and experienced supply chain professionals alike.**

Make critical decisions under pressure. Negotiate with suppliers. Navigate executive pressure. Invest in AI-driven resilience. See how every choice ripples through your business.

---

## 🚀 Quick Start

### Installation
No installation required. This is a single HTML file that runs completely offline.

1. **Download** `operation_lifeline.html`
2. **Open** it in any modern web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge)
3. **Click** "Start Simulation" and begin

That's it. No internet, no backend, no setup.

### System Requirements
- Modern web browser (2020+)
- No special plugins or extensions
- Works on desktop, tablet, and mobile
- ~2-3 MB of RAM
- Runs entirely offline

## 🎮 How to Play

### Game Flow (8 Phases)

#### 1. **Welcome Screen**
Learn your mission: you're the Chief Supply Chain Officer facing a crisis. Every decision matters. Get ready.

#### 2. **Company Profile**
Meet your company—randomly generated each playthrough:
- **Industry:** Electronics, automotive, pharma, fashion, food & beverage, semiconductors
- **Financials:** $900M–$3.6B annual revenue
- **Infrastructure:** 3–12 factories, 5–17 warehouses, 15–35 suppliers
- **Logistics:** 20–50 inventory days, 15–60 day lead times
- **Geography:** 2 primary regions (Southeast Asia, Europe, North America, South America)

**Why this matters:** Your company's size, industry, and supply chain structure determine what crises hit hardest and which solutions work best.

#### 3. **Crisis Trigger**
One of 8 crises strikes randomly:
- **Factory Fire:** Lose 40% manufacturing capacity
- **Supplier Bankruptcy:** Largest raw material supplier fails; alternatives cost 28% more
- **Port Strike:** 60% of shipments halted indefinitely
- **Cyberattack:** Systems encrypted; 5–7 days to recover; $2.3M ransom demand
- **Severe Flooding:** Two warehouses and a key supplier factory destroyed for 10+ days
- **Raw Material Shortage:** Global crisis; prices up 42%, availability at 60%
- **Political Conflict:** Trade sanctions block 40% of export markets
- **Shipping Delay Cascade:** Container ship sunk; port congestion triples lead times

Each crisis shows:
- **Description:** What happened and why it matters
- **Urgency:** Critical or High
- **Business Impact:** Immediate metrics (Cost, Inventory, Profit, Delivery Speed, Customer Satisfaction)

**Why this matters:** Different crises require different strategies. There's no one "right" answer.

#### 4. **War Room Decision (Choose 3 of 6 Actions)**
You have 6 options. Pick exactly 3 to execute immediately. Each has trade-offs:

| Action | Timeline | Risk | Use When |
|--------|----------|------|----------|
| **Activate Emergency Suppliers** | 24 hrs | Medium | You need capacity fast, willing to pay premium |
| **Negotiate Customer Extensions** | 12 hrs | Low | You need time and can risk customer frustration temporarily |
| **Implement Demand Reduction** | 6 hrs | Low | You want to stabilize quickly, accept lower revenue |
| **Secure Emergency Financing** | 48 hrs | High | You need cash to absorb costs, can handle debt |
| **Redirect Shipments** | 12 hrs | Medium | You have inventory but wrong location/customer |
| **Launch Customer Communication** | 4 hrs | Low | You want to minimize satisfaction damage through transparency |

**Why this matters:** Your three choices cascade through the rest of the crisis. Cost control, delivery performance, and customer satisfaction all depend on what you decide here.

#### 5. **Supplier Negotiation (4 Rounds)**
You're negotiating with your largest backup supplier for emergency capacity.

**Each round presents:**
- A negotiation scenario
- 3 dialogue choices reflecting different approaches (aggressive, collaborative, transactional)
- Immediate feedback on how the supplier reacted

**Tracked Metrics:**
- **Trust (0–100):** Relationship health; affects future willingness to help
- **Price Index (100+):** Cost multiplier for emergency orders
- **Lead Time (10+ days):** How fast they can deliver

**Why this matters:** 
- High trust, low price, short lead time = you got a good deal
- Low trust, high price, long lead time = you'll regret this later
- Supplier relationships are long-term assets; treat them that way

**Strategy Tips:**
- Collaborative approaches build trust but cost more upfront
- Aggressive approaches save money now but burn bridges
- "Partnership" language often unlocks better terms

#### 6. **CEO Boardroom (5 Strategic Questions)**
The board is breathing down your neck. Each question tests your crisis management philosophy:

1. **Customer Retention:** Do you cave to pressure or hold the line? (Hint: there's often a middle path)
2. **Talent Retention:** How do you keep your team from leaving during chaos?
3. **Timeline Transparency:** Your recovery plan is 8 months. Board wants 4. How do you respond?
4. **Compliance & Ethics:** You discover forced labor in your supply chain. What's your move?
5. **Post-Crisis Priority:** What matters most: speed, resilience, profitability, or automation?

**Scoring:**
- Points range 0–10 per question
- Low scores (1–3): Reactive, short-term thinking
- Medium scores (4–7): Balanced approach
- High scores (8–10): Strategic, values-aligned decision-making

**Why this matters:** Leadership decisions separate good crisis managers from great ones. They also reveal your company's culture and values under pressure.

#### 7. **AI Strategy Investment**
The board approved budget for 2 AI investments. Each has different business impact:

| Investment | Payoff | Best For |
|------------|--------|----------|
| **Demand Forecasting AI** | Predict demand 12 weeks out; reduce stockouts 65%, overstock 22% | Volatile demand, fast-changing markets |
| **Inventory Optimization** | Real-time reordering across 500+ SKUs; free up $45M cash | High inventory carrying costs |
| **Supplier Risk Monitoring** | Monitor supplier health in real-time; detect crises 87% faster | Complex supply chains, geopolitical exposure |
| **Warehouse Vision AI** | Computer vision inventory; reduce audit time 78%, improve picking 44% | Large warehouses, manual processes |
| **Procurement Copilot** | AI assistant for RFQs, supplier scoring, contract negotiation; save 56% cycle time | High procurement volume, sourcing complexity |

**Why this matters:** Different AI tools solve different problems. Choosing well positions you to handle future crises better.

#### 8. **Final Dashboard**
Comprehensive performance breakdown:

**Overall Crisis Score (0–100):**
- 85+: "You handled this like a pro. Promote immediately."
- 70–84: "Solid crisis management. A few blind spots, but strong leadership."
- 55–69: "You survived. There are lessons here. Sharpen your strategy."
- <55: "Gaps exposed. Invest in risk monitoring and supplier diversity."

**Individual Metrics (each 0–100):**
- **Cost Control:** How well you managed the financial hit
- **Delivery Performance:** Meeting customer deadlines despite chaos
- **Customer Satisfaction:** How happy your customers are after the crisis
- **Supply Chain Resilience:** Long-term strength; helped by AI investments
- **Negotiation Score:** Supplier trust and deal quality
- **Leadership Decisions:** Executive decision-making quality

**Feedback Sections:**
- **Biggest Mistakes:** What went wrong and why
- **Best Decisions:** What you nailed
- **Expert Recommendation:** Personalized advice based on your performance
- **Lessons Learned:** Takeaways for the next crisis (or real life)

**Replay Button:**
Every playthrough is unique. Click "Run Another Simulation" to get a new company, new crisis, and new challenges.

- **Premium dark theme:** Enterprise dashboard aesthetic (no bright colors, no amateur vibes)

## 💡 Strategy Tips

### War Room (Phase 4)
- **Early in crisis?** Activate Emergency Suppliers + Negotiate Customer Extensions + Launch Communication
- **Cash-strapped?** Demand Reduction + Customer Extensions + Customer Communication
- **Inventory-heavy?** Redirect Shipments + Emergency Financing + Demand Reduction
- **Delivery-focused?** Emergency Suppliers + Emergency Financing + Redirect Shipments

### Supplier Negotiation (Phase 5)
- **Collaborative language** ("Let's find a fair middle ground") builds trust and unlocks better terms
- **Aggressive ultimatums** ("Take it or find another buyer") save money short-term but poison the relationship
- **Partnership framing** ("We'll increase volume 30% in exchange for priority access") is powerful if backed up later

### CEO Boardroom (Phase 6)
- **Questions 1–2 (Retention):** The "right" answer involves understanding people and trade-offs, not just compliance
- **Question 3 (Timeline):** Present multiple scenarios instead of defending one timeline
- **Question 4 (Ethics):** Immediate action, even with costs, scores best. Ethics matter in crisis management.
- **Question 5 (Post-Crisis):** "Rebuild resilience" scores higher than "return to normal" or "cut costs"

### AI Strategy (Phase 7)
- **If you over-rotated on Cost Control:** Invest in Inventory Optimization + Demand Forecasting
- **If delivery was your weakness:** Supplier Risk Monitoring + Demand Forecasting
- **If you had supplier issues:** Supplier Risk Monitoring + Procurement Copilot
- **General hedge:** Demand Forecasting + Inventory Optimization (most broadly useful)

## 📊 Understanding Your Score

Your **Overall Crisis Score** is a composite of six factors:

```
Overall Score = 
  (Cost Control % + Delivery % + Satisfaction % + Resilience %) / 4  +
  (Leadership Score × 0.2) +
  (Supplier Trust × 0.15)
```

**What it means:**
- **0–40:** You survived, but just barely. Major gaps in crisis preparation.
- **40–55:** Middling performance. Some good decisions, some bad ones. Inconsistent leadership.
- **55–70:** Above average. You handled most situations well. Maybe one or two blind spots.
- **70–85:** Strong performance. Clear strategic thinking. Mostly sound decisions.
- **85–100:** Exceptional crisis management. You're a top-tier CSCO.

**Key insight:** You can't optimize for everything. If you prioritize customer satisfaction, cost control probably suffered. If you focus on cost, delivery speed might have taken a hit. This is realistic.

## 🎓 Learning Outcomes

After playing, you'll understand:

### Supply Chain Concepts
- **Lead time:** How long it takes to get goods; affects inventory strategy
- **Inventory days:** Safety stock buffer; affects cash flow
- **Supplier diversity:** Why depending on one supplier is risky
- **Cost-service trade-off:** You can't have low cost, fast delivery, and happy customers simultaneously

### Crisis Management
- **Speed vs. Strategy:** Fast decisions have long-term costs; strategic decisions take time
- **Stakeholder management:** Employees, customers, suppliers, board—they all have different needs
- **Negotiation:** Relationships matter; aggressive tactics win short-term battles but lose long-term wars
- **Ethics under pressure:** Compliance matters even (especially) during crises

### Decision-Making Under Uncertainty
- **Trade-offs:** Every choice has costs and benefits; there's no perfect move
- **Information gaps:** You'll never have perfect information; decide anyway
- **Risk tolerance:** Some solutions are safe but slow; others are fast but risky
- **Scenario planning:** Thinking through "what if" before crisis hits

### Technology & AI
- **AI's role in supply chains:** Monitoring, forecasting, optimization—not replacement
- **ROI of different tools:** Different AI investments solve different problems
- **Risk mitigation:** Technology can help but doesn't eliminate risk

## ❓ FAQ

**Q: Is this game realistic?**  
A: Yes. Every crisis, every decision, every trade-off is based on real supply chain scenarios. The crises are inspired by actual events (Fukushima, Suez Canal blockade, global chip shortage, COVID-19 lockdowns, etc.). The decisions and metrics reflect how real supply chain teams operate.

**Q: Do I need supply chain experience to play?**  
A: No. The game is designed for complete beginners. Every phase explains concepts before asking for decisions. That said, experienced supply chain professionals will also enjoy the realism and complexity.

**Q: Why doesn't X strategy win every time?**  
A: Because crises are complex. A strategy that works great for a factory fire (diversify suppliers quickly) might backfire for a political sanctions crisis (diversification doesn't matter if half your markets are blocked). The game rewards adaptability, not formula.

**Q: Can I change the game mechanics or add scenarios?**  
A: The HTML file is human-readable. If you know React and JavaScript, you can fork it and add your own crises, questions, or metrics. The code is modular and well-commented.

**Q: How long does a playthrough take?**  
A: 8–12 minutes for someone in a hurry, 20–30 minutes if you read everything and think through decisions.

**Q: What happens if I make all bad decisions?**  
A: You survive with a low score (probably 20–40). There's no "lose" condition; you always make it through the crisis. The question is how well you navigate it.

**Q: Is there a "right" answer to the boardroom questions?**  
A: Not exactly. The game rewards strategic, values-aligned thinking over reactive short-term moves. But even "low-scoring" answers teach valuable lessons about why certain approaches struggle in crises.

**Q: Can I replay and try the same company/crisis again?**  
A: No; randomization happens on each replay. But with 8 crises and multiple randomized companies, you'll get different challenges frequently.
